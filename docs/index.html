Calculation method and units
Travel emissions (kg) = Σ vehicle round trips × distance (km) × travel emission factor (g/km) ÷ 1,000

The emission factor applies to one vehicle. CO₂, CO, NOₓ and PM are calculated separately. Equal round-trip counts produce equal travel emissions under the shared distance and emission factors.

Idling fuel (L) = Σ vehicle round trips × idling time (min) ÷ 60 × fuel consumption rate (L/h)

Additional idling fuel uses Dasara round trips and max(0, Dasara idling time − normal idling time). It isolates the longer waiting time; it is not the difference between total normal and Dasara fuel consumption when round-trip counts differ. Fuel consumption is reported separately from emissions.

The default 42.5 km comprises 10 km to the area, 22.5 km around popular streets and 10 km home. The sightseeing distance is an assumption around DD Urs Road, Ramaswamy Circle, Golden Statue Circle, Vanivilas Road and the Chamundi Hills foothills, not a surveyed route.

Units: distance in km; idling time in min; emission factors in g/km per vehicle; fuel consumption rates in L/h per vehicle; total emissions in kg; daily emissions in kg/d; fuel consumption in L. Counts describe vehicle round trips, not unique vehicles.

Daily changes produce relative traffic weights. Normal and Dasara period counts are allocated to whole round trips by vehicle type, preserving the totals. The same distance, vehicle mix assumptions and emission factors apply each day. Slow movement, acceleration, cold starts, fuel and emission-standard differences, non-exhaust particles and exposure are outside this simple model. PM is unspecified exhaust particulate matter.

Data sources and assumptions

Travel emission factors

Venkataramana, Fauzie & Naveen (2018), Table 1 ↗. Historical factors for the four vehicle classes. The paper’s older registration inventory is not used as current traffic data.

Idling fuel consumption

Kumar, Singh, Sharma & Chalumuri (2015), Table 8 ↗, CSIR–CRRI researchers; the uploaded paper was checked directly. Measured fuel use converts to 0.144 L/h for four-stroke motorcycles, 0.240 L/h for two-stroke three-wheelers, 0.540 L/h for MPFI petrol cars and 0.900 L/h for diesel buses. These reference technologies are not a representative sample of the current Mysuru fleet. The same table reports diesel-car alternatives of 0.564 L/h for direct injection and 0.618 L/h for indirect injection.

Registered vehicles

Times of India (March 2026) ↗, reporting March 2025 registrations from Transport Minister data; ETAuto republication ↗. The four categories sum to 1,624,214. The original geographical coverage and Assembly data have not been independently audited. Registrations do not establish round-trip counts.

Normal traffic context

Laxmiprasad, Hameed & Lokesh (2020), pp. 14–15 ↗. CCTV observations at Cheluvamba Hospital junction gave daily ranges of 62,217–66,246 passages in April, 57,106–58,120 in June and 57,776–62,710 in December. These historical junction passages are not used as current citywide round trips.

Emission test definitions

ARAI, Indian Emissions Regulations (September 2021) ↗. The uploaded booklet was checked directly. Printed pages 85–86 provide idling concentration limits, not mass emission rates. These limits are not used as numerical inputs.

The initial 15% fleet participation, 40% Dasara increase, 11 d period, route, waiting times and daily pattern are editable assumptions. The Dasara increase includes additional festival activity; a separate incoming-car estimate is not added.
